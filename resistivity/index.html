The coastal sandstone around Gosford and Woy Woy hides a lot of surprises. What looks like solid ridgeline can sit above deeply weathered shale lenses, and the salt wedge in estuaries like Brisbane Water shifts further inland than most expect. We run electrical resistivity and vertical electrical sounding (VES) surveys across the Central Coast NSW to map these contrasts before a single excavator bucket hits the ground. The method works because clay, fresh sandstone, brackish groundwater, and dry sand each read differently when we inject current into the ground. On a recent job near Erina, the resistivity profile caught a buried paleochannel that boreholes missed—saving the developer six figures in redesign. We pair the field data with MASW profiling when shear-wave velocity is also needed for site classification under AS 1170.4.

Resistivity tells you where the water is, where the rock is, and where the clay is—before you dig. On the Central Coast, that's often the difference between a straightforward cut and a dewatering headache.

Technical details of the service in Central Coast NSW

The Central Coast NSW sits on a mix of Narrabeen Group sandstone, residual clay, and Quaternary alluvium along the coastal plain. Decades of subdivision around Tuggerah and Wyong have pushed construction into areas with variable fill thickness and perched water tables. VES gives us a fast way to map bedrock depth across a whole site, not just at isolated borehole locations. We use a Schlumberger array because it handles the deep targets common in this region—often 20 to 40 metres to competent rock—while keeping lateral resolution tight. The crew runs multiple soundings per day, and we process the data with 1D inversion followed by 2D resistivity sections when the geology is laterally patchy. Every dataset includes apparent resistivity curves, modelled layer parameters, and a site plan with sounding locations tied to MGA coordinates. For road projects where compaction control matters, the same crew can run sand cone density testing in parallel, so you get earthworks QA and subsurface profiling from one mobilisation.

Risks and considerations in Central Coast NSW

The most common mistake on Central Coast NSW sites is assuming uniform sandstone at depth based on a single borehole. We have seen jobs where the designer placed strip footings on what they thought was shallow rock, but the resistivity section showed a 6-metre-deep trough of saturated clay directly under the pad corner. Differential settlement showed up within two years. Another problem is saline intrusion mapping near lakefront developments around Lake Macquarie and Tuggerah Lakes. Without resistivity lines run perpendicular to the shoreline, the cut-off depth for aggressive soil exposure classification ends up as guesswork. Our reports flag these transitions explicitly. We deliver interpreted sections with layer resistivities correlated to expected lithology, so the geotechnical engineer can assign soil parameters with confidence. When the resistivity contrast is weak, we recommend supplementary CPT soundings to ground-truth the boundaries.

Our services

Our resistivity work on the Central Coast NSW covers three core applications. Each job gets a dedicated crew with a senior geophysicist on site during data acquisition.

Depth-to-Bedrock VES Surveys

Schlumberger array soundings on a grid pattern to map rockhead topography across the site. We deliver contoured bedrock surface maps ready for earthworks estimating.

Groundwater and Salinity Mapping

Resistivity transects to locate the freshwater-saltwater interface and map perched aquifers in coastal alluvium. Used extensively for MAR projects and landfill siting.

Fill Characterisation and Buried Feature Detection

2D resistivity imaging to delineate uncontrolled fill thickness, locate buried channels, and identify old service trenches where records are missing.

Top questions

How deep can VES investigate on a typical Central Coast sandstone site?

With a Schlumberger spread we routinely reach 40 to 60 metres depth on sandstone terrain. The limiting factor is the site footprint—you need roughly three times the target depth in cable length. We can push to 80 metres on large industrial lots near Warnervale where space is available.

What does an electrical resistivity survey cost in the Central Coast region?

Most jobs fall between AU$840 and AU$1,680 depending on the number of soundings, the array type, and how far the crew needs to travel from our mobilisation point. A single VES sounding with a short report starts at the lower end. A full grid with 2D inversion and an interpretive report moves toward the upper end.

Can you run resistivity lines on steep bush blocks around Kincumber or Bouddi?

Yes, but access dictates the array layout. On steep terrain we often switch to shorter Wenner spreads or run the line along the contour rather than upslope. The crew carries lightweight cable and electrodes, so we can work on sites where a drill rig cannot reach.

How long until we get the results after the field survey?

Preliminary depth-to-bedrock profiles go out within 48 hours. The full interpretive report with inverted sections, layer parameters, and correlation to any available borehole logs takes five to seven working days.
